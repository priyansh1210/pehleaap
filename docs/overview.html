pehleaap

Teaching phone voice bots when to talk on Indian Hinglish calls, so they stop cutting people off mid-sentence and stop going quiet when they should answer.

"pehle aap" = "after you" 8 kHz phone audio 8-week project · ends ~20 Dec 2026

The problem

Why bots get Hinglish phone calls wrong

A voice bot has to guess, many times per call, whether the caller has finished speaking. Most bots guess from silence: "they stopped, so it's my turn." On Indian phone calls that guess is often wrong, for four reasons that come from how people speak Hinglish:

Pauses before the verb

main kal payment… kar dunga

Hindi puts the verb last, so people pause in the middle of a sentence. The bot thinks they're done.

"Haan" can mean two things

haan… haan

Sometimes it's "I'm listening, go on". Sometimes it's a real answer. The bot must tell them apart.

"Ek minute"

ek minute, card dhoondh raha hoon…

The caller asked the bot to wait. A long silence is expected; the bot shouldn't fill it.

Reading out numbers

98765… 43210

People pause between groups of digits. A pause there doesn't mean the number is finished.

On top of that, phone lines are bad audio: 8 kHz sound with half the detail of a normal recording, compression codecs, lost packets, background noise and speakerphone echo. Most turn-taking models are trained on clean audio and fall apart here.

What we want to achieve

Four things by 20 December

Deliverable A

An open training recipe and models

Code anyone can rerun to train a turn-taking model on 8 kHz Hinglish phone audio.

Deliverable B

A public benchmark

Real recorded conversations from 40+ speakers, with checked labels and a scoring script. Test speakers are never in training.

Deliverable C

Measured cost

How many milliseconds each decision takes on a normal CPU, and how many calls one core can handle.

Deliverable D

A live demo

A web voice call through a simulated phone line, with a switch between a standard model and ours.

How we describe it: "a training recipe plus a benchmark that a company can rerun on its own call data." Not a finished product. Every result says what test set it was measured on.

The four questions the bot must answer

working nowHas the caller finished?End-of-turn (EOT). This is what eot_v0 and eot_v1 do.
week 4Is this an interruption or just "haan"?Barge-in: should the bot stop talking, or carry on?
week 4Did they ask me to wait?Hold intent: "ek minute", "ruko", "hold karo".
week 4What kind of "haan" was it?Backchannel type: "go on" versus "I agree / that's right".

How everything works

The complete framework

Three connected loops. 1 · Learn: audio goes through a fake phone line and teaches the model. 2 · Check and use: the model is tested and runs in the demo. 3 · Our recordings: real conversations get labelled and feed both of the others.

done happening now coming Scroll sideways on a phone.

The plan's final system adds a tiny always-on listener that runs every 10 ms and wakes the bigger model only when something happens (a pause, or the caller speaking over the bot), plus a decision layer with "patient / balanced / snappy" settings. Both come in weeks 4-5.

Inside a live call

How one "is it my turn?" decision happens

Caller goes quiet

The phone audio arrives at 8 kHz, just as on a real call.

Wait 0.2 s

A voice detector confirms it's a real pause, not a breath.

Model listens

It hears the last 8 seconds: words, pitch, rhythm, how the voice trailed off.

Score 0 to 1

"How sure am I the caller is finished?" Takes 29-57 ms on one CPU core.

Decide

Above 0.5: the bot replies. Below: it keeps listening. After 3 s of silence it replies anyway.

Words used here

Glossary

End-of-turn (EOT)
The moment a caller has finished and it's the bot's turn to talk.
Cut-in
The bot talks while the caller still had more to say. The worst mistake.
Missed end
The caller had finished, but the model said "not yet", so the bot replies late.
Barge-in
The caller talks over the bot to interrupt it.
Backchannel
Short "haan", "hmm", "achha" that mean "I'm listening", not "my turn".
TTS
Text-to-speech: computer-generated voices. Smart Turn's Hindi data is all TTS.
8 kHz
Phone-call audio quality: about half the detail of a normal recording.
Codec
How a phone network compresses voice (G.711, AMR, GSM, Opus). Each one damages the sound differently.
Smart Turn v3.2
An open turn-taking model from Pipecat. Our starting point, and the main baseline.
Fine-tuning
Taking an existing model and training it further on our kind of audio.
ONNX / int8
A portable model file. int8 is a compressed version that runs about twice as fast.
FLEURS
Google's free dataset of real people reading sentences aloud, including Hindi.
AUC
How well a model ranks finished above unfinished, ignoring the exact threshold. 1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is guessing.
Dev / test set
Dev: held-out data used to pick the best checkpoint. Test: kept untouched for the final score.
